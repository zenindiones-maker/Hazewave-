# HAZEWAVE ACADEMIC LIBRARY — Master Specification v1

## 1. Mission

HAZEWAVE Academic Library is the university-level publishing program of HAZEWAVE.

Its long-term subject is the history of human beings through sound: listening, voice, rhythm, instruments, space, ritual, work, memory, performance, technology, recording, reproduction, broadcasting, synthesis, sampling, software, networks and generative systems.

It is not a finite “history of music” series.

It is an expandable scholarly library capable of producing hundreds or thousands of volumes whenever the evidence, disciplinary importance and research depth justify that scale.

The governing idea is:

> Human history can be reread through the sounds people heard, made, organized, remembered, transmitted, recorded and transformed.

## 3. Academic identity

The Academic Library is independent from entertainment outputs.

NEANDERCAUS, interactive works, music releases and films may reuse the research, but the academic book must remain valid if all other Hazewave media are ignored.

A volume cannot cite “the series says so”.

It must cite the underlying scholarship, archive, artifact, recording, dataset, experiment or primary document.

## 4. Publication families

### HAZEWAVE ACADEMIC MONOGRAPHS

Focused arguments about a defined problem.

Typical scale:
- 70,000–120,000 words;
- approximately 250–450 pages;
- external peer review;
- explicit thesis and method.

### HAZEWAVE HISTORIES

Large chronological, regional or thematic histories.

Typical scale:
- 90,000–180,000+ words;
- approximately 350–700+ pages;
- multiple expert reviewers;
- extensive maps/timelines.

### HAZEWAVE REFERENCE

Atlases, encyclopedias, catalogues, chronologies, annotated source books and research companions.

Scale is determined by reference function, not narrative length.

### HAZEWAVE SOURCE EDITIONS

Critical editions of public-domain or licensed historical texts, scores, catalogues, transcriptions, archival collections and research datasets.

### HAZEWAVE TEACHING EDITIONS

University-facing editions derived from scholarly volumes.

May include:
- chapter objectives;
- lecture maps;
- discussion questions;
- listening exercises;
- source criticism;
- assignments;
- instructor notes.

Teaching editions cannot simplify away material scholarly disagreement.

## 5. Infinite-library model

Volumes are not forced into one book per century or one book per civilization.

The library expands across independent dimensions:

```text
TIME
× REGION
× PEOPLE / CULTURE
× SOUND PRACTICE
× INSTRUMENT
× TECHNOLOGY
× GENRE
× INSTITUTION
× MEDIUM
× ARCHIVE / CORPUS
× THEORETICAL PROBLEM
```

A single historical interval can therefore sustain many books without artificial padding.

Example:

```text
RECORDED SOUND 1877–1910
├── recording technologies
├── performance changes
├── cylinder industries
├── disc industries
├── archives and preservation
├── regional markets
├── voice
├── popular repertoires
├── classical repertoires
├── colonial recording
├── intellectual property
└── listening culture
```

## 6. Canonical first volume

**HW-ACAD-001**

# Música de Antes da Pré-História
## O Primeiro Ouvinte
### Som, consciência e experiência humana antes da música organizada

Core proposition:

> The deep history of music begins with the human act of listening, not with the oldest surviving instrument.

The book is human-centered.

It does not narrate the origin of life as its primary subject.

It places a modern/future critical lens inside early-human material and sensory conditions while keeping all reconstruction epistemically separated from evidence.

## 7. Bitemporal Lens

All immersive academic volumes may use the Hazewave **Bitemporal Lens**.

### Inside-time layer

The reader experiences:
- weather;
- darkness;
- terrain;
- proximity;
- movement;
- body;
- voice;
- tools;
- materials;
- social presence;
- sound.

### Analytical layer

The book asks:
- what evidence exists?
- how secure is dating?
- what alternative interpretations exist?
- what concepts are ours rather than theirs?
- what does the archaeological record erase?
- what remains unknowable?

The historical people never receive modern thoughts merely because the narrator has them.

## 8. Academic integrity invariants

A Hazewave academic volume must never:

- fabricate a citation;
- invent an archaeological object;
- invent an archive location;
- present AI-generated text as a primary source;
- present AI-generated imagery as documentary evidence;
- present an artistic reconstruction as a historical recording;
- collapse scholarly disagreement into certainty;
- use a living tradition as a direct fossil analogue for prehistory;
- treat “music”, “art”, “religion” or “ritual” as universally identical categories without methodological discussion;
- silently convert possibility into fact.

## 12. University-readiness target

A volume intended for university use should contain, as applicable:

- explicit research problem;
- thesis;
- historiography;
- methodology;
- chapter-level arguments;
- primary source engagement where available;
- peer-reviewed scholarship;
- counterarguments;
- limitations;
- notes;
- bibliography;
- glossary;
- subject index;
- name/place/site index;
- figure list;
- source/rights list;
- stable edition/version;
- external review record;
- correction policy.

A page count alone never establishes scholarly quality.

## 13. Peer review

Before scholarly publication:

- minimum two external subject-matter reviewers for a standard monograph;
- more reviewers when the book crosses several specialized fields;
- conflicts of interest recorded;
- author response matrix required;
- unresolved major objections documented;
- editorial decision separated from marketing pressure.

Where feasible, at least one reviewer should be close to the specific geographic/cultural domain under discussion.

## 14. Geographic and cultural scope

HAZEWAVE rejects a single Western ladder of musical progress.

The library is chronological where chronology is meaningful, but globally braided.

Research may involve:

- Africa;
- Southwest Asia / Middle East;
- South Asia;
- Central/North Asia;
- East Asia;
- Southeast Asia;
- Oceania/Pacific;
- Europe;
- North America;
- Mesoamerica/Caribbean;
- South America.

Coverage is evidence-driven.

“Global” never means superficially mentioning every region in every volume.

## 15. Living cultures

Living musical traditions are contemporary.

They are not evidence that prehistoric people “must have done the same thing”.

When relevant:
- use local terminology;
- seek community/practitioner scholarship;
- document colonial archives and collection histories;
- respect restricted/sacred knowledge;
- secure rights for recordings;
- distinguish present practice from historical continuity claims.

## 16. AI policy

AI may assist:

- discovery;
- search expansion;
- transcription drafts;
- translation drafts;
- metadata normalization;
- comparison;
- diagram prototyping;
- editorial QA.

AI may not be the final authority for:
- historical facts;
- citations;
- translations of critical passages;
- cultural interpretation;
- artifact identification;
- rights status;
- peer review.

All important AI-assisted claims must be checked against human-readable sources.

## 17. HAZE Listening Labs

- HAZE_INTERPRETATION

A listener must always know what kind of object they are hearing.

## 18. WAVE visual evidence

WAVE distinguishes:

- artifact photograph;
- site photograph;
- archival image;
- archaeological drawing;
- scholarly diagram;
- map;
- modern reconstruction;
- analytical visualization;
- speculative illustration;
- AI-generated concept visualization.

Caption language must expose the category.

## 19. Correction and version policy

Books are durable scholarly objects but not infallible.

Each edition receives:
- edition identifier;
- publication date;
- revision date;
- correction log;
- known-issues page;
- digital version hash when practical.

Substantive corrections remain visible.

## 20. Final quality principle

HAZEWAVE does not ask the reader for trust without inspection.

The scholarly ideal is:

> Experience the past.  
> Analyze the music.  
> See the uncertainty.  
> Follow the source.  
> Check us yourself.
