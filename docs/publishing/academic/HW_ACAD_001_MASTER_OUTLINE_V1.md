# HW-ACAD-001 — Professional Master Outline v1
# MÚSICA DE ANTES DA PRÉ-HISTÓRIA
## Volume 1 — O Primeiro Ouvinte

## Narrative objective

Construct a university-level historical argument through a sequence of evidence-bound lived scenes.

The reader does not watch “prehistoric humans” from outside.

The reader repeatedly enters a research-locked human situation, acts inside it, then exits into modern analysis.

## Dramatic question

> When does sound stop being merely part of the world and become something humans intentionally organize, remember and share?

## Book movement

### MOVEMENT I — BEFORE WE CALL IT MUSIC
The world supplies information through sound.

### MOVEMENT II — THE BODY ANSWERS
Humans discover that their own bodies and voices alter the acoustic world.

### MOVEMENT III — THE WORLD ANSWERS BACK
Spaces and objects transform intentional sound.

### MOVEMENT IV — WE BEGIN TO SHARE TIME
Repetition, synchronization and memory create social structure.

### MOVEMENT V — THE THRESHOLD
We confront the limits of defining the first music.

## Chapter production map

### CHAPTER 01 — THE FIRST SOUND THAT MATTERED
**Story function:** establish the bitemporal lens.
**Immersive problem:** perception before classification.
**Academic question:** what can “meaningful sound” mean in a Paleolithic human context?
**Gate:** no exact site/time until research dossier locks the opening coordinate.

### CHAPTER 02 — HEARING BEFORE SEEING
**Story function:** sound becomes spatial information.
**Questions:** localization, occlusion, darkness, distance.
**WAVE:** spatial acoustic diagram.
**HAZE:** localization listening lab.

### CHAPTER 03 — THE SOUND OF DANGER
**Story function:** sound changes behavior before the source is visible.
**Questions:** environmental information, predator/prey uncertainty, alarm.
**Constraint:** do not invent specific predator encounters without site evidence.

### CHAPTER 04 — THE SOUND OF HOME
**Story function:** recurring human activity creates a recognizable sound world.
**Questions:** domestic space, repeated occupation, hearth-centered activity where evidenced.
**Evidence focus:** spatial archaeology / combustion structures.

### CHAPTER 05 — BREATH
**Story function:** the closest recurring sound is inside/next to the body.
**Questions:** effort, emotion, proximity, rhythm.
**Constraint:** physiological facts can be modern analysis; symbolic meaning requires evidence.

### CHAPTER 06 — THE CRY
**Story function:** voice causes immediate social response.
**Questions:** distress/contact/attention; human universals vs cultural interpretation.
**Constraint:** “first cry” is a thought experiment, never a historical event claim.

### CHAPTER 07 — HANDS
**Story function:** intentional impact can be repeated.
**Questions:** clap/body percussion; evidence problem when sound leaves no artifact.

### CHAPTER 08 — FEET
**Story function:** locomotion can become shared pulse.
**Questions:** movement, dance inference, substrate sound.
**Constraint:** separate plausible movement from evidence of dance.

### CHAPTER 09 — VOICE BEFORE SONG
**Story function:** repeated/pitched vocal behavior enters analytical ambiguity.
**Question:** where do call, speech-like vocalization and song diverge conceptually?

### CHAPTER 10 — CALLING ACROSS DISTANCE
**Story function:** sound extends social reach.
**Questions:** terrain, visibility, distance, vocal projection.

### CHAPTER 11 — IMITATION
**Story function:** humans can reproduce environmental sounds.
**Questions:** mimicry, learning, symbolic inference.
**Gate:** high caution; distinguish capacity from prehistoric usage claims.

### CHAPTER 12 — THE WORLD ANSWERS
**Story function:** echo introduces delayed self-produced sound.
**Questions:** echo/reverberation as affordance.

### CHAPTER 13 — ACOUSTIC SPACES
**Story function:** landscape becomes part of sonic behavior.
**Questions:** caves, rock shelters, cliffs, open terrain.
**Evidence:** archaeoacoustic measurements + archaeological context.
**Gate:** acoustic property ≠ ritual purpose.

### CHAPTER 14 — STONE AGAINST STONE
**Story function:** work sound becomes sonically noticeable.
**Questions:** lithic production acoustics; tool noise vs selected sound.

### CHAPTER 15 — THE SOUND OBJECT
**Story function:** an object may be struck/shaken/blown for its acoustic result.
**Questions:** intentionality and preservation.
**Bridge:** prepares later instrument archaeology.

### CHAPTER 16 — REPETITION
**Story function:** recurring events become expected.
**Questions:** cognitive prediction, pattern and temporal structure.

### CHAPTER 17 — MANY BODIES, ONE TIME
**Story function:** synchronization becomes group experience.
**Questions:** entrainment, coordinated work/play, evidence limits.

### CHAPTER 18 — MEMORY IN SOUND
**Story function:** repeated sonic patterns can cue recognition and expectation.
**Questions:** memory without notation; oral/embodied transmission.
**Gate:** do not assert specific songs without evidence.

### CHAPTER 19 — THE SOCIAL SOUND
**Story function:** sound structures proximity, attention and belonging.
**Questions:** domestic/social contexts; group cohesion as hypothesis.

### CHAPTER 20 — RITUAL WITHOUT INVENTING RITUAL
**Story function:** confront the temptation to call every unusual site “ritual”.
**Questions:** evidence standards for ritual interpretation.
**Value:** teach archaeological reasoning directly.

### CHAPTER 21 — WHEN A SOUND BECOMES A CHOICE
**Story function:** intentional sonic selection becomes central.
**Questions:** affordance, material choice, repetition, context.

### CHAPTER 22 — MUSIC: A WORD FROM THE FUTURE
**Story function:** expose our own category.
**Questions:** cross-cultural definitions; “music” as analytical term.

### CHAPTER 23 — THE FIRST MUSIC WE CANNOT HEAR
**Story function:** missing evidence becomes part of the argument.
**Questions:** preservation bias; ephemeral voice/body traditions.

### CHAPTER 24 — THE FIRST MUSIC WE CAN TOUCH
**Story function:** transition toward material evidence.
**Questions:** early sound-producing artifacts / secure instrument evidence to be selected by later research.
**Gate:** exact case studies selected only after global evidence review.

### CHAPTER 25 — THE THRESHOLD
**Story function:** close without inventing a first song.
**Conclusion:** music history begins earlier than the oldest surviving instrument, but archaeology constrains what can be claimed.
**Forward:** Volume 002.

## Character / community strategy

Do not invent one immortal Paleolithic family spanning tens of thousands of years.

Use **Research Windows**.

Each Research Window is tied to a narrower time/place/context and contains a small fictional composite community.

The bitemporal “we” is continuous across windows, but the historical people are not.

This preserves narrative continuity without collapsing prehistory into one culture.

## Recommended number of Research Windows

Volume 001 should probably use 4–8 deeply researched windows rather than dozens of shallow locations.

The final count must follow the evidence and the thesis.

A future research tranche should deliberately seek geographic diversity rather than defaulting to western European caves.

Each immersive scene is scripted before prose as:


This is the bridge between academic research and professional narrative writing.

## Opening scene standard

The first scene should not be written as a generic “cave at dawn.”

Research first selects a real or defensible archaeological window.

Only then does the literary opening get drafted.

The opening must demonstrate the method in miniature:

1. sound precedes explanation;
2. the reader acts inside the scene;
3. one sound changes behavior;
4. the text freezes;
5. evidence reinterprets what just happened;
6. the reader returns and hears it differently.

## Volume-level acceptance criteria

The master manuscript passes only if:

- the story has a real progression rather than 25 essays;
- each chapter changes the reader's model of sound;
- scenes feel lived but remain epistemically honest;
- geographic/time windows are not collapsed into a generic prehistory;
- evidence can be audited claim by claim;
- modern consciousness is kept in the narrative lens rather than falsely attributed to past people;
- the HAZE companion and WAVE figures teach rather than decorate;
- reviewers from relevant disciplines have challenged the strongest claims.
