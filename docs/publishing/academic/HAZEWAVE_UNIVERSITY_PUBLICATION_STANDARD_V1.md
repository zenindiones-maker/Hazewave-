# HAZEWAVE University Publication Standard v1

## 1. Goal

This standard defines the target for a Hazewave book intended to be credible for university reading lists, libraries, seminars and course adoption.

It does not claim that any university must adopt the book.

Adoption remains a decision of professors, departments, libraries and institutions.

## 2. Scholarly identity

A university-targeted volume must be recognizable as scholarship before it is recognizable as brand content.

Required:
- named authors/editors;
- academic responsibility;
- research question;
- thesis or scholarly purpose;
- methodology;
- disciplinary positioning;
- citations;
- bibliography;
- peer review;
- stable edition;
- correction mechanism.

## 3. Recommended manuscript scale

For a standard major Hazewave academic monograph:

- target: 90,000–140,000 words;
- common final extent: roughly 320–520 pages;
- no artificial padding;
- shorter/longer allowed when argument requires it.

Reference works may be much larger.

## 4. Front matter

Depending on volume:

- title page;
- copyright / edition data;
- authors / editors;
- scientific/editorial board;
- peer-review statement;
- acknowledgements;
- note on terminology;
- note on dates/calendars;
- note on transliteration;
- note on AI-assisted workflow;
- list of figures;
- list of maps;
- list of tables;
- list of audio companions;
- preface;
- introduction.

## 5. Scholarly body

A strong monograph normally includes:
- problem statement;
- historiography/state of research;
- methods;
- definitions;
- evidence chapters;
- argumentative synthesis;
- competing interpretations;
- limitations;
- conclusion.

The Bitemporal Lens may shape presentation but never replaces these functions.

## 6. Notes and citation style

The project may choose Chicago Notes & Bibliography or another discipline-appropriate style.

Consistency is mandatory.

Citations should include exact page/section/object/track locators whenever practical.

## 7. Bibliography architecture

Separate when useful:
- primary sources;
- archaeological reports;
- archival sources;
- books;
- journal articles;
- theses/dissertations;
- museum catalogues;
- recordings;
- scores;
- datasets;
- audiovisual sources;
- web-native scholarly resources.

## 8. Indexes

For large scholarly volumes:

- subject index;
- people/author index;
- place/site index;
- instrument/practice index where valuable.

An index is an academic navigation tool, not decoration.

## 9. Glossary

Required when specialist terms are frequent.

Local/emic terminology should be preserved and explained.

Do not replace culturally specific terms with generic English/Portuguese categories if precision is lost.

## 10. Tables and figures

Every figure carries:
- figure ID;
- creator/source;
- date if known;
- rights status;
- evidentiary category;
- caption;
- citation;
- accessibility alt text in digital editions.

## 11. Maps

Maps identify:
- coordinate basis;
- source;
- date layer;
- uncertain boundaries;
- historical vs modern names.

Modern national borders should not silently imply prehistoric/past political geography.

## 12. Musical examples

Musical analysis may use:
- staff notation;
- rhythmic grids;
- cyclical diagrams;
- spectrograms;
- waveform/onset displays;
- tuning plots;
- transcription;
- movement alignment;
- annotated audio.

Every representation explains its limits.

## 13. Audio companion

The book remains academically complete without network access.

Audio adds evidence/analysis rather than hiding essential argument.

Stable chapter URLs should outlive temporary platform links.

## 14. Peer review minimum

Standard monograph:
- at least two external reviewers.

Cross-disciplinary/high-risk work:
- additional specialist review.

Possible reviewer domains:
- archaeology;
- paleoanthropology;
- archaeomusicology;
- ethnomusicology;
- musicology;
- acoustics;
- organology;
- history;
- anthropology;
- regional/cultural expertise.

## 15. Scientific/editorial board

HAZEWAVE Books should eventually maintain a real external advisory/scientific board.

Board expectations:
- disclosed affiliations;
- relevant expertise;
- conflicts policy;
- no ceremonial name-only appointments;
- term/review policy.

## 16. Conflict of interest

Authors, editors and reviewers disclose:
- close collaboration;
- financial relationships;
- supervisory relationships;
- institutional conflicts;
- personal conflicts relevant to review.

## 17. Research ethics and cultural material

When dealing with living communities:
- permissions where required;
- restricted/sacred content controls;
- attribution;
- community-sensitive terminology;
- data/recording restrictions;
- repatriation/colonial provenance context where relevant.

## 18. AI disclosure

The publication discloses material AI assistance.

At minimum distinguish:
- search/discovery;
- language editing;
- translation assistance;
- image generation;
- audio generation;
- data analysis;
- transcription.

AI cannot be listed as the scholarly authority for a historical claim.

## 19. Plagiarism / source integrity

Required controls:
- original-source verification;
- quote verification;
- paraphrase audit;
- citation completeness;
- duplicate-text review;
- permission check for long quotations/images.

## 20. Publication identifiers

Professional publication should obtain, as applicable:
- ISBN for editions;
- library/cataloguing metadata;
- DOI for book/chapter/research objects where infrastructure supports it;
- ORCID links for contributors where available;
- stable canonical URLs.

Identifiers do not prove scholarly quality, but they improve discoverability and citation.

## 21. Accessibility

Digital edition target:
- semantic reading order;
- alt text;
- accessible tables;
- keyboard navigation for web companions;
- transcripts for audio;
- captions for video;
- non-color-only meaning;
- scalable text.

## 22. Teaching package

A university adoption pack may include:
- 12-week syllabus map;
- 16-week syllabus map;
- chapter learning objectives;
- discussion questions;
- primary-source exercises;
- listening exercises;
- slide-ready figures;
- instructor bibliography;
- assessment prompts.

Answer keys or instructor-only materials are separated where appropriate.

## 23. Library durability

Academic assets should be preserved beyond the website UI.

Maintain:
- print master;
- PDF/A or equivalent archival copy where appropriate;
- EPUB;
- source metadata;
- checksum;
- edition manifest;
- repository/archive copy.

## 24. Correction policy

Classifications:

- TYPOGRAPHIC
- METADATA
- CITATION
- FACTUAL_MINOR
- FACTUAL_MAJOR
- INTERPRETIVE
- RIGHTS
- RETRACTION_LEVEL

Major corrections trigger a new version/edition record.

## 25. Academic readiness gate

Before labeling a volume “university edition”, verify:

```text
METHOD = PASS
HISTORIOGRAPHY = PASS
CLAIM_LEDGER = PASS
SOURCE_RESOLUTION = PASS
MUSICOLOGY_DEPTH = PASS
EXTERNAL_REVIEW = PASS
AUTHOR_RESPONSE = PASS
FACT_CHECK = PASS
RIGHTS = PASS
ACCESSIBILITY = PASS
INDEX = PASS
BIBLIOGRAPHY = PASS
VERSION_MANIFEST = PASS
```

No marketing copy can override a failed gate.
