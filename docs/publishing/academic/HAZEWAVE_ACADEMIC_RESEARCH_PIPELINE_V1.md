# HAZEWAVE Academic Research Pipeline v1

## 1. Objective

This document defines how a Hazewave academic book moves from an idea to a defensible scholarly manuscript.

The sequence is evidence-first.

Narrative quality matters, but no scene is allowed to drive the evidence backward.

## 2. Lifecycle

```text
PROPOSAL
→ SCOPE LOCK
→ RESEARCH QUESTIONS
→ HISTORIOGRAPHIC MAP
→ SOURCE DISCOVERY
→ SOURCE TRIAGE
→ SOURCE CORPUS
→ CLAIM-EVIDENCE LEDGER
→ RESEARCH WINDOWS
→ MUSIC DOSSIERS
→ SCENE DOSSIERS
→ OUTLINE
→ PILOT CHAPTER
→ INTERNAL AUDIT
→ FULL DRAFT
→ EXPERT REVIEW
→ REVISION MATRIX
→ FACT CHECK
→ COPYEDIT
→ RIGHTS / PROVENANCE CHECK
→ PROOF
→ PUBLICATION
→ POST-PUBLICATION CORRECTIONS
```

## 3. Proposal gate

A proposal must define:

- book ID;
- working title;
- academic field(s);
- primary question;
- secondary questions;
- tentative thesis;
- chronology;
- geography;
- exclusions;
- target readership;
- known source bases;
- likely research risks;
- likely cultural/rights risks;
- relation to other Hazewave volumes.

A proposal is rejected when its scope is only “tell everything about X”.

## 4. Scope lock

Before deep research, define what the book will not attempt.

For HW-ACAD-001, primary exclusions include:
- origin of life as a narrative subject;
- complete evolutionary history of hearing;
- universal reconstruction of prehistoric consciousness;
- claims of a recoverable “first song”.

Exclusions protect depth.

## 5. Research-question hierarchy

Questions are typed:

### PRIMARY
The book cannot succeed without answering or reframing them.

### SECONDARY
Support a primary question.

### CONTEXTUAL
Provide necessary background.

### DISCOVERY
Questions that emerge during research.

### UNANSWERABLE / LIMIT QUESTION
Explicitly tests what the evidence cannot establish.

## 6. Historiographic map

Before drafting arguments, map the field:

```text
SCHOOL / APPROACH
KEY AUTHORS
FOUNDATIONAL WORKS
RECENT REVIEWS
MAJOR AGREEMENTS
MAJOR DISAGREEMENTS
METHODS
KNOWN BIASES / LIMITS
OPEN QUESTIONS
```

The goal is to understand the scholarly conversation before adding Hazewave’s voice.

## 7. Source discovery

Discovery can use:
- bibliographic databases;
- university catalogues;
- journal indexes;
- citation chaining;
- museum databases;
- archaeological repositories;
- national libraries;
- archives;
- dissertations/theses;
- conference proceedings where useful;
- expert recommendations.

Discovery sources are not automatically evidence sources.

## 8. Source triage

Each source is classified for:

- relevance;
- authority;
- date;
- methodology;
- primary/secondary status;
- peer-review status;
- sample/corpus quality;
- geographic fit;
- chronological fit;
- language;
- known criticism;
- access stability.

## 9. Source corpus

The canonical corpus stores bibliographic metadata and research notes.

No uncited paraphrase should become a manuscript claim merely because it exists in a researcher’s notes.

## 10. Claim-evidence ledger

Each major claim receives:

```text
CLAIM_ID
CHAPTER
CLAIM
TYPE
STATUS
SOURCES
LOCATORS
SUPPORTING_NOTE
COUNTEREVIDENCE
ALTERNATIVE_INTERPRETATION
CONFIDENCE
REVIEWER
PUBLIC_CITATION
```

A claim cannot move to VERIFIED because several websites repeat it.

## 11. Triangulation

High-impact claims should, where the evidence allows, combine independent lines:

- artifact/context;
- dating;
- materials;
- spatial evidence;
- acoustics;
- iconography;
- written primary source;
- audio recording;
- ethnomusicological research;
- experimental archaeology.

More sources do not automatically mean stronger evidence if all derive from the same unsupported claim.

## 12. Research Windows

A Research Window is a bounded historical coordinate used for immersive scenes.

Required fields:

```text
WINDOW_ID
TIME_RANGE
GEOGRAPHY
SITE / LANDSCAPE
ARCHAEOLOGICAL CULTURE / POPULATION
DATING BASIS
ENVIRONMENT
MATERIAL CULTURE
SUBSISTENCE
FIRE
SPATIAL ORGANIZATION
SOUND-RELEVANT EVIDENCE
DIRECT EVIDENCE
INFERENCES
OPEN QUESTIONS
PROHIBITED CLAIMS
CORE SOURCES
```

Research Windows prevent “generic prehistory”.

## 13. Music dossiers

Whenever the narrative encounters a substantial musical practice, create a dedicated dossier.

Required dimensions:

- local names;
- performers;
- social context;
- instruments;
- materials;
- technique;
- acoustics;
- tuning/pitch;
- rhythm;
- form;
- ensemble structure;
- improvisation;
- transmission;
- movement/dance;
- language relationships;
- historical development;
- regional variants;
- recordings;
- transcriptions;
- analytical controversies.

## 14. Archaeological inference ladder

For prehistoric sound:

```text
PHYSICAL POSSIBILITY
↓
MATERIAL AFFORDANCE
↓
CONTEXTUAL ASSOCIATION
↓
USE-WEAR / DEPOSITION / SPATIAL EVIDENCE
↓
SOUND-PRODUCING USE
↓
INTENTIONAL SOUND USE
↓
MUSICAL USE
↓
PERFORMANCE PRACTICE
```

The text must not jump steps without evidence.

## 15. Analogy controls

Ethnographic analogy can help formulate hypotheses.

It cannot demonstrate that prehistoric groups shared the same meaning or practice.

Every analogy states:
- why it is being used;
- structural similarity;
- historical distance;
- cultural difference;
- what the analogy cannot prove.

## 16. Experimental archaeology

Each experiment receives:

```text
EXPERIMENT_ID
QUESTION
MATERIALS
RECONSTRUCTION ASSUMPTIONS
VARIABLES
PROCEDURE
MEASUREMENTS
RESULTS
REPEATABILITY
LIMITATIONS
INFERENCE_ALLOWED
INFERENCE_FORBIDDEN
```

## 17. Acoustic modeling

Acoustic models state:
- geometry source;
- measurement/model assumptions;
- material assumptions;
- environmental parameters;
- uncertainty;
- validation method;
- whether the output is measured, simulated or interpreted.

## 18. Drafting gate

No chapter prose begins until:
- chapter question exists;
- minimum corpus threshold is met;
- scene window is bounded;
- central claims have candidate evidence;
- major disagreement is known;
- known anachronism risks are listed.

## 19. Pilot chapter

Before writing the full book, produce one complete chapter including:
- immersive scene;
- analytical bridge;
- deep evidence section;
- music analysis;
- HAZE lab;
- WAVE figure;
- notes;
- bibliography;
- public source page mockup.

External readers should test the method, not only the prose.

## 20. Internal audit

Auditors ask:
- Is this actually supported?
- Is the cited source being represented accurately?
- Is chronology current?
- Is the geographic framing correct?
- Is a hypothesis disguised as fact?
- Is terminology anachronistic?
- Is the musical analysis deep enough?
- Are non-English/local sources missing?
- Does the scene overclaim a mind-state?

## 21. Expert review

Review is issue-driven.

Reviewers receive:
- manuscript;
- source map;
- list of strongest claims;
- list of disputed claims;
- requested domain focus.

They are not asked merely “did you like it?”

## 22. Author response matrix

Every material review point becomes:

```text
REVIEW_ID
REVIEWER_COMMENT
SEVERITY
AUTHOR_RESPONSE
ACTION
MANUSCRIPT_LOCATION
EVIDENCE_ADDED
STATUS
```

## 23. Fact-check lock

Before layout:
- citations resolve;
- quotes match originals;
- dates match sources;
- object IDs resolve;
- names/transliterations consistent;
- captions accurate;
- maps checked;
- audio metadata checked;
- rights status checked.

## 24. Publication evidence package

A published academic volume retains:

```text
FINAL_MANUSCRIPT
BIBLIOGRAPHY
CLAIM_LEDGER
FIGURE_LEDGER
AUDIO_LEDGER
RIGHTS_LEDGER
REVIEW_SUMMARY
AUTHOR_RESPONSE_MATRIX
CORRECTION_POLICY
VERSION_MANIFEST
```

Not all private reviewer identities must be public, depending on review model, but editorial accountability must remain documented.

## 25. Stop conditions

Research pauses when:
- a central claim cannot be supported;
- source access is inadequate;
- rights are unresolved;
- cultural review is required;
- geography is being generalized without evidence;
- reviewers identify a causal flaw in the thesis.

Deadline pressure does not convert these into PASS.
