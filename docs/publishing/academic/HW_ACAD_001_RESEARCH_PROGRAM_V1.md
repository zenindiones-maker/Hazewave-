# HW-ACAD-001 Research Program v1
# Música de Antes da Pré-História — O Primeiro Ouvinte

## 1. Mission

Build the evidence base for a university-level book about the earliest human experience of sound before formal musical systems are archaeologically visible.

The book asks:

> How might human listening, body sound, voice, space, material resonance, repetition, memory and social coordination create the conditions from which musical behavior emerges?

## 2. Narrative rule

We experience historically constrained scenes from inside.

We carry present/future critical questions.

We never claim that prehistoric people possessed our concepts.

## 3. Research domains

The research program requires coordinated work in:

- Paleolithic archaeology;
- paleoanthropology;
- archaeomusicology;
- archaeology of sound;
- archaeoacoustics;
- experimental archaeology;
- environmental acoustics;
- cognition;
- rhythm/entrainment research;
- vocal communication;
- anthropology of music;
- organology;
- philosophy of music;
- historiography of prehistoric music research.

## 4. Primary questions

### Q1 — LISTENING
What can scholarship responsibly say about the informational importance of sound in early-human environments?

### Q2 — BODY
What kinds of intentionally produced body sound are physiologically possible, and what can archaeology actually demonstrate?

### Q3 — VOICE
What evidence can constrain discussion of vocal behavior before surviving notation/recording?

### Q4 — SPACE
How should cave, rock-shelter and open-air acoustics be analyzed without treating acoustic properties as proof of ritual?

### Q5 — MATERIALS
What evidence distinguishes accidental/tool sound from intentionally selected sound-producing objects?

### Q6 — REPETITION
What can cognitive/experimental research contribute to understanding pattern, prediction and synchronization without projecting modern musical structures backward?

### Q7 — SOCIALITY
What is supportable about sound in coordination, identity, ceremony, work and group behavior?

### Q8 — MUSIC
Which definitions of music are analytically useful for deep prehistory, and what do they conceal?

## 5. Research Windows

The final book should likely use several deeply researched windows.

Candidate windows are not selected because they are famous.

Selection criteria:

- quality of archaeological record;
- chronology;
- sound-relevant evidence;
- environmental reconstruction;
- availability of primary reports;
- methodological value;
- geographic diversity;
- ability to test a chapter question.

Do not default to western European caves.

## 6. Window selection matrix

Score candidates on:

```text
DATING_QUALITY
SITE_DOCUMENTATION
SPATIAL_DATA
MATERIAL_PRESERVATION
ENVIRONMENTAL_DATA
SOUND_RELEVANCE
PRIMARY_REPORT_ACCESS
RECENT_SCHOLARSHIP
INTERPRETIVE_RISK
GEOGRAPHIC_CONTRIBUTION
```

Scoring helps triage; it does not mechanically select sites.

- source classification;

## 13. Review requirements

HW-ACAD-001 likely needs expertise across:
- Paleolithic archaeology;
- archaeomusicology/sound archaeology;
- acoustics/archaeoacoustics;
- musicology/ethnomusicology;
- cognition/rhythm.

Not every reviewer must cover every domain.

## 14. Stop conditions

Do not write a confident scene when:
- the chosen window lacks evidence;
- chronology is unstable;
- core source is inaccessible;
- environmental assumptions are unverified;
- scene requires unsupported social organization;
- “ritual” is doing unexplained work;
- sound function is inferred from capability alone.

## 15. Final book promise

The reader should finish understanding two things simultaneously:

1. humans almost certainly experienced rich sound worlds long before music becomes materially visible;
2. the farther we travel beyond surviving evidence, the more disciplined our language must become.

That tension is the intellectual center of Volume 001.
