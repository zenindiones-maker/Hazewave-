# HAZEWAVE BOOKS — Editorial & Educational System v1

## Role

**HAZEWAVE BOOKS** is the publishing arm of the Hazewave universe.

Editorial ownership belongs to **WAVE Studio** because books are authored visual/editorial objects: writing, research synthesis, diagrams, maps, illustration, photography, typography, layouts, covers, print/digital editions and educational design.

**HAZE Studio** owns the sonic companion layer: music, reconstructed/interpretive sound examples, narrated listening guides, soundtrack cues, pronunciation, audio essays, sound design and audiobook production.

The books are not marketing material for the animated series. They are a serious educational collection that can stand on its own while sharing research, canon and audiovisual assets with NEANDERCAUS.

## A journey through the human history of sound

## Narrative device

The didactic hook is **time travel through listening**.

The narrator uses a plural first-person point of view: we are contemporary music obsessives suddenly placed inside a historical sound world.

We do not arrive as omniscient historians.

We arrive asking:

- What can we hear?
- What materials can make sound here?
- Where are people gathering?
- What survives archaeologically?
- What do we actually know?
- What are scholars inferring?
- What sounds are completely lost?
- What musical ideas from this place/time still echo later?

The books should make the reader feel transported while keeping every historical claim auditable.

Working narrative principle:

> We travel through time as people obsessed with music, listening before judging.

## Educational contract

The series must be useful as a learning resource, not just atmospheric writing.

Every chapter separates:

- **VERIFIED** — supported by strong archaeological, documentary or scholarly evidence;
- **PROBABLE** — plausible and supported, but incomplete;
- **DISPUTED** — meaningful scholarly disagreement;
- **SPECULATIVE_SCENE** — literary reconstruction used to make the era experiential;
- **HAZE_INTERPRETATION** — modern sonic interpretation created by Hazewave.

A speculative scene can be vivid.

It cannot silently become a factual claim.

## Global-history rule

The collection must not reduce the history of music to a single Western chronology.

History is chronological but braided across regions.

Required research coverage, when relevant to the era:

- Africa;
- Southwest Asia / Middle East;
- South Asia;
- Central / North Asia;
- East Asia;
- Southeast Asia;
- Oceania / Pacific;
- Europe;
- North America;
- Mesoamerica / Caribbean;
- South America.

Living cultures must not be described as leftovers of an ancient past.

Traditional practices that continue today are contemporary living practices, even when they preserve old techniques.

## Research stack

Each volume should be built from a documented evidence stack.

Priority order:

1. peer-reviewed archaeology / archaeomusicology;
2. ethnomusicology and organology;
3. primary historical texts where applicable;
4. museum catalogues and collections;
5. archaeological reports;
6. university research;
7. national archives / libraries;
8. specialist monographs;
9. reliable recordings and field documentation;
10. community / culture-bearer sources for living traditions.

Popular summaries may help discovery but should not be the sole support for central claims.

## Cultural review rule

Before publication, chapters about living or historically marginalized cultures should receive contextual review appropriate to the claim.

The production system must flag:

- sacred/restricted practices;
- culturally sensitive objects;
- contested identity labels;
- colonial naming;
- disputed origin claims;
- uncertain reconstructions;
- imagery that could falsely imply authenticity.

## Visual evidence rule

WAVE must distinguish:

- actual artifact photograph;
- archaeological drawing;
- historical illustration;
- modern reconstruction;
- diagram;
- speculative concept art;
- AI-generated visualization.

An AI-generated image may never be captioned or framed as a photograph of a historical artifact or event.

## Sonic evidence rule

HAZE must distinguish:

- surviving historical recording;
- documented modern performance tradition;
- reconstruction using documented instruments;
- acoustical experiment;
- Hazewave interpretation;
- fully speculative composition.

A modern HAZE production inspired by an era must never be labeled as “what the era sounded like” without qualification.

## Book experience

Each major chapter follows a recognizable learning rhythm.

### 1. ARRIVAL

A short immersive literary scene.

We enter the place/time through sound first.

### 2. WHERE / WHEN

Map, date range, environment, societies and evidence context.

### 3. WHAT WE CAN ACTUALLY KNOW

Artifacts, texts, acoustics, surviving traditions and evidence limitations.

### 4. THE SOUND-MAKING WORLD

Voice, body, instruments, materials, spaces and ensembles.

### 5. WHY PEOPLE MADE SOUND

Ritual, work, play, identity, storytelling, war, court, religion, ceremony, dance, entertainment, communication or other evidenced roles.

### 6. LISTENING CLOSELY

A HAZE audio companion.

### 7. BUILD / TEST / EXPERIENCE

Safe educational activity, acoustics experiment, rhythm exercise, material demonstration or digital simulation where appropriate.

### 8. PARALLEL WORLDS

What was happening in other regions during the same broad period.

### 9. WHAT TRAVELS FORWARD

Influences, continuities, exchanges, transformations and explicit uncertainty about claimed lineages.

### 10. WHAT WE STILL DO NOT KNOW

Open questions.

### 11. SOURCES

Readable notes plus full scholarly references.

## Shared motif

The same musical DNA used by NEANDERCAUS may appear in the books' audio companions.

That gives the reader a recurring listening experiment:

```text
one motif
→ body percussion
→ stone
→ wind / bone
→ strings
→ ensemble
→ mechanical reproduction
→ tape
→ synth
→ sample
→ digital
→ generative model
```

This is an artistic continuity device, not a claim of direct historical lineage.

## Initial library architecture

The exact volume count should emerge from research rather than be artificially fixed.

Working collection:

### VOLUME 0 — HOW TO LISTEN TO THE PAST
Evidence, silence, reconstruction, archaeology, acoustics and the limits of knowing ancient sound.

### VOLUME 1 — BODY, BREATH, STONE
Deep prehistory; body sound; natural acoustics; early material sound-making; what can and cannot be inferred.

### VOLUME 2 — BONE, WOOD, SKIN, AIR
Early instruments and material resonance across regions.

### VOLUME 3 — SETTLEMENT, RITUAL, ENSEMBLE
Sound in increasingly complex communities and built spaces.

### VOLUME 4 — ANCIENT SOUND WORLDS
Multiple ancient musical systems studied in parallel rather than collapsed into one “ancient music” chapter.

### VOLUME 5 — COURTS, TEMPLES, ROADS, OCEANS
Regional classical/sacred/court traditions, mobility, exchange and transmission.

### VOLUME 6 — CONTACT, CONQUEST, DIASPORA, HYBRIDITY
Migration, empire, forced movement, trade, colonization and musical transformation.

### VOLUME 7 — PRINT, MACHINES, PUBLICS
Notation, publishing, industry, instruments, urban audiences and changing circulation.

### VOLUME 8 — THE RECORDED SOUND
Phonograph, records, microphones, radio and the moment sound becomes reproducible media.

### VOLUME 9 — ELECTRICITY, TAPE, STUDIO
Amplification, magnetic recording, multitrack production and the studio as instrument.

### VOLUME 10 — SYNTHESIS, DUB, HIP-HOP, SAMPLE
Electronic sound, studio experimentation, remix cultures, turntablism and sampling.

### VOLUME 11 — DIGITAL SOUND, SOFTWARE, INTERNET
MIDI, digital recording, compression, DAWs, files, networks and global distribution.

### VOLUME 12 — MODELS, VOICES, GENERATIVE MUSIC
Machine learning, source separation, synthesis, voice conversion, generative systems, provenance and the emerging AI era.

### VOLUME 13 — HAZEWAVE
A transparent making-of volume explaining how HAZE, WAVE and NEANDERCAUS reinterpret the research rather than pretending to reproduce the past.

This list is a research scaffold, not a final publication schedule.

## Quality gate

A book is not ready because the prose sounds convincing.

Minimum publication gates:

- central claims have sources;
- uncertainty is explicit;
- regional framing is not misleading;
- living cultures are treated as living;
- speculative scenes are labeled;
- image provenance is known;
- sonic companion labels interpretation vs evidence;
- citations are complete;
- accessible reading order exists;
- print/digital proofs pass;
- external subject review is complete for high-risk claims.

## Relationship to NEANDERCAUS

The books and animated series share a research universe but serve different functions.

**Books explain and teach.**

**NEANDERCAUS dramatizes and performs.**

**HAZE lets us hear interpretations.**

**WAVE lets us see, read and navigate the history.**

Together they form a multi-medium history of humanity through sound.
